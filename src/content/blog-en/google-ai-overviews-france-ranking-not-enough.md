Google's AI Overviews went live in France on July 22, and they're changing how a business needs to think about visibility.

**On the queries where they trigger, organic clicks drop by 61% for sites left out of the Overview, while sites that get cited gain as much as 35%.**

## Ranking Doesn't Tell You Much Anymore

Most business owners still track one thing: their Google ranking. Page one, top three, ideally the top spot. To me, that's where the logic breaks down. Since July 22, Google no longer just lists links for the queries in question. It answers the question directly, at the top of the page, ahead of the usual results. And that answer cites some sites, not others.

So a site can hold its ranking and still lose the click. This isn't a theory, it's already measured: 61% fewer clicks on queries where the Overview appears, except for the sites Google chooses to cite (Source: [Abondance](https://www.abondance.com/20260722-2640402-lancement-officielle-ai-overviews-france.html), a leading French SEO publication, 2026). The French rollout lands two years after other markets, held up by a dispute over how AI systems compensate news publishers. Few businesses have adjusted their tracking since.

France's late start has one upside: the window is still open. Two years of watching other markets gave everyone plenty of time to prepare. Almost nobody did. On the ground, plenty of owners are still finding out about the Overview by stumbling across it on their own brand search.

## Why Google Answers Instead of Sending a Click?

This rollout isn't a simple display tweak. Google changed what it does with the question you ask it. It used to index pages and rank them, in the order it judged most relevant to the query. The user clicked, read, compared a few results if needed. Now, on a portion of queries, it reads several pages, pulls out an answer, and shows it directly, at the top, ahead of the usual list. The user gets an answer without leaving Google. The click becomes optional, not necessary.

This shift has a name: generative engine optimization. In practice, that means writing content the AI can lift straight into its answer, not just content the algorithm ranks well. Two different exercises. A site can excel at one and be absent from the other, because its structure doesn't make it easy to extract: no isolated stat, no direct answer at the top of a section, everything buried in a general introduction.

What Google pulls first are self-contained passages: an answer in one or two sentences, understandable without reading the rest of the page. An article built as one long narrative, with no section that directly answers a specific question, gives it far less to cite. It's not a quality issue. It's a format issue.

A site used to rank by matching the right keywords with enough authority. Now Google also has to judge whether a specific passage answers the question on its own, unambiguously. Two sites with equally good content can get treated differently depending on which one wrote its answer in an extractable way.

## What This Doesn't Change

Being cited in an Overview doesn't mean giving up on classic ranking work. A site missing from the first few pages has no shot at being picked up by the Overview anyway: Google pulls its material from pages it already judges relevant and well built, not at random. Traditional SEO stays the foundation. What's changed is that it's no longer enough on its own. You're adding a layer of measurement, not replacing the one that was already there.

That distinction matters, because the temptation is to throw everything out and start over on a new buzzword. So, no: no full rebuild, no rushed purchase of a miracle new tool. Just one more indicator to watch, alongside the one you're already tracking.

## Getting Cited, Not Just Ranked

Picture a commercial cleaning company, sitting on page one for a search related to SEO audits, for months. Its organic traffic starts slipping with no alert going off in the usual tools: its ranking hasn't moved. What's changed is that Google now answers the question directly, and cites a competitor in its Overview, not them.

An owner who only watches their ranking won't see it coming. The tracking tool will show a stable score for months, while real traffic drops month after month. That gap between the two measures is exactly what the AI Overview just created.

That's where real visibility plays out today: being the source the Overview chooses to cite, not just the link a user might click. A ranking check alone won't catch it. It keeps showing a steady number while real traffic erodes underneath. So the question isn't just "am I ranking well?" anymore. It's "if Google answers in my place, does it cite my site, or a competitor's?"

The gap between the two business owners comes down to one habit: one of them regularly checks what Google actually shows for their priority searches, the other trusts their ranking-tracker dashboard. The first saw the shift the day it happened. The second will find out in their quarterly review, wondering why quote requests slowed down while nothing, on paper, had moved.

## What To Do About It

- **Check your citation in AI Overviews for your key searches**, not just your Google ranking. Search your own priority queries and note whether an Overview appears, and who it cites. Ten minutes covers the ten searches that matter most to your business.
- **Find the searches where competitors already get cited**, to prioritize which pages to rework first. A query where the Overview consistently cites the same competitor deserves attention before the others: that's where the lost opportunity is already measurable.
- **Structure your content to answer the question directly**, the format Overviews pick up most easily. One or two sentences at the top of a section, before the detail. The rest of the piece still has its place, for readers who want to go deeper.

A monthly check is enough for now. The pace will pick up as Google extends Overview coverage to more searches, but nothing today suggests checking weekly. A steady, kept-up habit beats a one-off check that gets forgotten the following month.
